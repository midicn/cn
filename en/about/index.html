What this station is for

This is the single home for Chinese music. Where other stations touch a Chinese tradition, opera or folk song, they give one comparison line and hand the reader over to this station — nothing is written twice.

The five sections

  • Tuning systems and modes — pentatonic and seven-note scales, temperament and the gong-mode relationships, and how they are used in actual pieces.
  • Opera — the main vocal traditions, role types and accompanying ensembles, and how melody relates to set tunes.
  • Instrumental music — the different ways ensembles are organised: civil and martial pieces, silk-and-bamboo, wind-and-percussion, and string ensembles.
  • Folk song — arranged by region and by kind, explaining where a song is sung and how.
  • Living traditions — how a tradition has travelled, what variants exist, and where it stands today.

What each entry looks like

  • It first explains how the thing is used in Chinese music — concept, structure, setting.
  • Then it cross-refers to the instruments and theory stations: seen from inside the Chinese context and from general theory, the same idea is often described differently, and both readings are needed.
  • Playable examples prefer openly usable archive recordings.
  • In both Chinese and English; Chinese terms keep their Chinese spelling, with the English giving a transliteration or an explanation.

How to read it

1. Start from a tradition — to learn about opera, enter through opera, not by walking through every instrument. 2. Read alongside — for Chinese instruments, keep the instrument atlas open next to it. 3. Watch the vocabulary — a word in the Chinese context is not always the same word in general theory.

Where it begins

All five sections are being added over time. Tuning systems and modes come first, since both opera and instrumental writing lean on them; folk song is the largest part and will arrive region by region. :::
